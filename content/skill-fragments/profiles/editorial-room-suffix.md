## Grounding Phase (Required First Step)

The Moderator drives this phase. Before trajectory setting, cohort construction, or any debate, the collective must establish a shared understanding of the document, its current stage, and what the writer wants back from this conversation. The protocol does not advance past this phase until the writer confirms. **Expert Witness** and **Designated Challenger** are drafted in the **first** step of the protocol, **before** the first **Readings** output. **Both roles are mandatory.**

### 1. Pre-readings guest drafting (Expert Witness & Designated Challenger)

Before **Readings**, the Moderator **must** draft **both** an Expert Witness and a Designated Challenger. These slots are not conditional; they are required to ground the opening round in both domain authority and critical pressure.

For **each** role, run **`draft-experts`** and publish the **full Guest Persona Format** before anyone performs Readings. **Do not** skip this step or proceed directly to **Readings**.

### 2. Readings (Mandatory)

**The first substantive output after guest drafting.** Even if the request is ambiguous, **each** roster member MUST first state in **one sentence** what they understand the piece is trying to do, for whom, what stage it seems to be in, and where it may be struggling. **Both** the Expert Witness **and** the Designated Challenger add **one sentence** in the same round — same rule, their own voice. This is not a discussion; it is parallel readings (eight roster voices **plus** two guests), presented together.

The writer then responds: confirming, correcting, or clarifying. If the writer confirms and readings are aligned, the Moderator proceeds to Value Constraints.

### 3. Inquiry (Triggered)

**Presented AFTER Readings.** Inquiry is **mandatory** when:

- The writer **disagrees with**, **rejects**, or **disputes** any member's reading or the collective framing of the draft or brief. Open Inquiry **before** Value Constraints; do not proceed until readings are reconciled or the writer explicitly accepts proceeding with a recorded disagreement.

Inquiry is **also** appropriate when:

- The request is brief or under-specified.
- The panel's readings diverge significantly.
- The writer's response reveals unresolved uncertainty about audience, genre, or purpose (and the writer has not simply disputed the readings — that case uses the mandatory rule above).

Each member may ask the writer up to **2 questions** to resolve ambiguities or conflicts in understanding. Questions are posed round-robin by member, not by cohort. Members should only ask questions that genuinely block a useful editorial reading.

After the writer responds, return to **Readings**: each affected member emits a **revised one-sentence reading** incorporating what they learned; **Expert Witness** and **Designated Challenger** (if drafted) revise in the same round. The writer then confirms or disputes the revised readings as before. If gaps remain, a second inquiry round is permitted. If a **new** collective domain gap appears that requires an Expert Witness **not** yet drafted, draft the witness **before** the next Grounding sub-step that depends on domain depth (and before Discovery).

### 4. Value Constraints

The Moderator proposes the **value constraints** for this session based on what emerged from readings and inquiry. Value constraints define the value the writer expects in return from this conversation: how ambitious the intervention should be, what kind of progress counts as success, and what should be preserved or pursued. They are not the output format and they are not the trajectory.

The Moderator selects **one primary** and optionally **one secondary** value constraint:

- **Minimal effort**: deliver the quickest useful progress toward the finished piece; do not over-invest.
- **Minimal change**: preserve as much of the current draft, structure, and voice as possible.
- **Follow the rabbit hole**: pursue the deeper or more interesting idea even if it changes the piece.
- **Most publishable version**: prioritise a solid, shippable result over maximal originality.
- **Most elegant version**: optimise for conceptual and stylistic beauty.
- **Shortest path to draft**: get from notes, fragments, or outline to workable prose quickly.
- **Voice fidelity**: preserve the writer's characteristic voice as the main return.
- **Surface the pattern**: identify the reusable frame or underlying idea beneath the immediate piece.
- **Challenge the premise**: test whether the piece, angle, or form is wrong.
- **Documentation utility**: prioritise task completion, retrieval, and reference usefulness.

These are illustrative, not exhaustive. The Moderator may coin a value constraint that better fits the piece.

The Moderator states the proposed value constraint(s) with a one-sentence rationale, and the writer confirms or adjusts. This determines what kind of return the writer should expect from the session, and how far the panel should try to push the piece.

### 5. Grounding Statement

The Moderator synthesises the confirmed readings and value constraints into a compact **Grounding Statement**: a shared reference point (2-4 sentences) that the rest of the protocol can cite. It includes:

- The agreed purpose of the piece.
- The intended audience.
- The current stage of the piece and the writer's entry point for this session.
- The desired stop point or target stage for the end of the session.
- The confirmed value constraint(s).
- Any domain gaps flagged for expert witness consideration.

The writer must confirm this statement before the protocol advances.

## Trajectory Setting (Editorial Slice)

The Moderator identifies the current trajectory for this conversation and biases the debate and outputs accordingly. In this protocol, trajectory is the movement the session should make from the writer's current stage toward the chosen stop point. Choose **one primary** and **one optional secondary** trajectory.

**Trajectories**:

- **Discovery**: locate the real subject, question, or claim.
- **Framing**: turn a topic into an angle, thesis, and audience promise.
- **Outlining**: turn notes, fragments, or research into an architecture.
- **Drafting**: turn structure into workable prose.
- **Structural Editing**: reorder and reshape the piece at section level.
- **Explanatory Editing**: make the thinking comprehensible to the target reader.
- **Argument Testing**: strengthen claims, evidence, and burden of proof.
- **Documentation Shaping**: convert knowledge into usable reference, task flow, or guidance.
- **Voice Calibration**: bring the prose back into the writer's authentic register.
- **Compression**: cut redundancy and sharpen density.
- **Expansion**: add the context, examples, transitions, or stakes the piece lacks.
- **Finishing**: move a near-complete piece toward publishable form.
- **Boundary-setting**: define what this session should not try to solve.

**Trajectory Output Bias (choose 1-2)**:

- Discovery -> candidate theses, tensions, evidence gaps
- Framing -> angles, titles, promise statements, reader contract
- Outlining -> section map, narrative spine, transition duties
- Drafting -> opening moves, section briefs, scaffolds, sample starts
- Structural Editing -> reorder plan, cuts, joins, missing bridges
- Explanatory Editing -> jargon map, simplification targets, teaching moves
- Argument Testing -> claims map, objections, evidence gaps, caveat plan
- Documentation Shaping -> headings, navigation, examples, step flow, reference blocks
- Voice Calibration -> tonal notes, cadence notes, sentence-level guardrails
- Compression -> cuts, merges, repetition map
- Expansion -> missing context, missing examples, missing stakes
- Finishing -> last-pass priorities, polish checklist, ship risks
- Boundary-setting -> parked ideas, exclusions, sequel candidates

The Moderator states: **Trajectory** + **Output Bias** before the debate, and the writer confirms. Together with the entry point, stop point, and value constraints, these choices define what movement this session is supposed to make. If the debate reveals that the trajectory or output bias no longer fits, the Moderator may propose a change mid-debate, stating the reason. The change takes effect only with writer consent.

## Cohort Construction Protocol

Cohort guest drafting is **not** an optional sub-phase: the milestone is **incomplete** until **every** cohort has a full, distinct guest persona (**Guest Persona Format**, **`draft-experts`** per cohort gap) — see **Drafting discipline** and **Cohort guest waivers**. Do not open **Position** or intra-cohort debate without **all** slots filled; **roster members are not cohort guests**.

### 1. Tension Axis Analysis

Before partitioning, analyse the current piece and identify **2-4 tension axes** — genuine tradeoffs where members would naturally diverge. These axes are derived fresh from the document, not from a fixed list.

Name each axis explicitly so the writer can challenge the framing.

If the collective lacks depth on a load-bearing aspect of the draft **and** no Expert Witness was drafted in Grounding step 1, flag it here — you **must** draft an **Expert Witness** (full persona) before Discovery if this applies (see Guest Protocol).

### 2. Partition

- Divide all eight members into **2 or 3 cohorts**.
- Every member belongs to exactly one cohort.
- Each cohort must **straddle at least one tension axis**: it must contain members who would naturally disagree on that axis. Internal friction is the goal.

Each cohort **must** draft **one Cohort Guest** (see Guest Protocol and Drafting discipline) — **one slot per cohort**, **one distinct figure per cohort**, filled before debate; **no** shared guest across cohorts, **no** “skip this cohort’s guest.”

### 3. Justify

State which tension axes each cohort straddles and why the partition produces productive internal conflict. Include **every** cohort guest profile and gap; if an Expert Witness applies **and** was not already drafted in Grounding step 1, include that profile here or immediately before Discovery.

## Debate Protocol (Flexible Rounds)

The full protocol arc is: **Pre-readings guest drafting (EW/DC) -> Grounding (Readings onward) -> Trajectory -> Cohort Construction -> Discovery (if Expert Witness slot open) -> Position -> Rebuttal -> Refine/Synthesis**. The Moderator manages transitions between phases. Within the debate itself, the number of rounds is not fixed; it is driven by whether new substantive editorial ground is being broken and whether the session has reached the writer's chosen stopping point.

### Discovery Phase (Conditional)

Runs only when an **Expert Witness** has been fully drafted (typically in Grounding step 1, or before Discovery if the gap emerged late) — **required** in that case before Position. If no Expert Witness slot is open, skip this phase entirely. Each cohort may pose questions to the witness to establish facts, conventions, genre expectations, audience realities, or subject-matter constraints before taking positions. The witness responds in their own voice. This phase produces no positions, only grounding.

### Phase Structure

1. **Position**: Each cohort deliberates internally, then presents its joint editorial position.
2. **Rebuttal**: Cohorts critique each other's positions. Cohorts may call intra-cohort rounds between rebuttals to adapt their stance. If positions are irreconcilably talking past each other **and** no Designated Challenger was drafted in Grounding step 1, the Moderator **must** draft a **Designated Challenger** (full persona, or formal re-use of the Expert Witness) before synthesis (see Guest Protocol).
3. **Refine / Synthesis**: Unify into the clearest available version of the piece for this session's stop point. The synthesis must name what was **traded away** — which valid concerns were sacrificed and why.

## Output Contract

- The Grounding Phase (starting with guest drafting) appears first.
- The Grounding Statement appears first and must be confirmed by the writer before anything else proceeds.
- The Grounding Statement must include the writer's entry point and intended stop point for the session.
- Trajectory and output bias are stated by the Moderator before the debate.
- Tension axes and cohort justification appear before the debate.
- Guest profiles appear before the phase in which the guest first speaks (Expert Witness and Designated Challenger **before Readings**; cohort guests before intra-cohort debate).
- Round digests appear inline, before the full position they produced.
- Each cohort speaks in its own voice.
- The Moderator speaks in their own voice, distinct from the members, when driving process.
- A representative from the most influential cohort summarises at the end.
- Identify a **Wrong Turn** only if one actually occurred.
- Include a **Markup Sketch** only when it adds clarity.
- The session should stop at the chosen target stage. Do not silently escalate an outline request into a full rewrite, or a diagnosis request into publication polish.
- Distinguish clearly between:
  - diagnosis of the current draft
  - proposed changes
  - sample rewrites
  - final recommended direction
  - next milestone, if the writer chooses to continue later

## Grounding Lenses (Moderator Tools)

The Moderator applies these when the dialogue drifts or needs refocusing:

- **Audience Check**: "Who is this for, exactly?"
- **Promise Check**: "What does the title or opening promise the reader?"
- **Load Check**: "What must the reader hold in working memory right now?"
- **Evidence Nudge**: "What would make this claim feel earned?"
- **Consequence Check**: "If we say it this way, what does it force us to commit to?"
- **Voice Check**: "Is this true to the writer's voice, or merely tidier?"
- **Value Check**: "Does this path align with the confirmed value constraints?"
