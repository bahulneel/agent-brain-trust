#### 3. Designated Challenger

**Trigger (primary)**: **Before Readings** — the Moderator flags that the topic or document will likely need a **named interlocutor** to stress‑test **opposing stakes**. Stakes include **operational** and **methodological** forks, not only “ideology”: verification vs speed, ordering vs safety, local simplicity vs global invariants, “does this compose?” vs “ship now,” and any **competing viable** reading of what must hold.

**Strong presumption**: If the brief implies **engineering or process decisions under uncertainty** (including verification, sequencing, interfaces, distributed behaviour), treat the primary trigger as **fired** unless the **Valid narrow exception** below applies.

**Valid narrow exception**: The user request is **purely** definitional or **purely** cosmetic with **no** tradeoff surface for the opening round — state that in one explicit sentence.

**Trigger (fallback)**: During **Rebuttal**, cohort positions are too incompatible to synthesise — they talk past each other — **and** no Designated Challenger was drafted before Readings.

**Timing**: When a trigger applies, you **must** designate a challenger (full persona, or an explicit hand‑off if the **Expert Witness** already drafted is taking this role) **before Readings** (primary) or **before Refine / Synthesis** (fallback only). Do not advance to synthesis while the deadlock condition holds and this slot is unfilled.

- When drafted before Readings, the challenger contributes **one sentence** in **Readings** (same rule as roster members). If the challenger appears only via the **Rebuttal fallback**, they **do not** retroactively join Readings; they enter for the critique before synthesis.
- The challenger may be the expert witness (if one was drafted) or a newly drafted figure — state which.
- During debate, the challenger delivers a focused critique of each cohort's position when the protocol calls for it (including the **fallback** path), identifying the shared assumption or framing error causing impasse.
- The challenger does not co-author the Synthesis but the Synthesis must respond to their critique when they have spoken in that role.
