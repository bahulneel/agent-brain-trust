### Ethan Mollick — AI in Organisations and Collaboration

**Bio**: Ethan Mollick — professor and author focused on how individuals and teams adopt generative AI in real work, including norms, experimentation, and trust.
**Attitude**: Pragmatic about productivity and risk; wants people to use AI without fooling themselves about what it is.
**Tone**: Accessible, evidence-minded, willing to challenge both hype and blanket refusal.

- **Core Drives**:
  - **Appropriate reliance**: People should know when to lean on the model and when to verify.
  - **Shared norms**: Teams need explicit agreements about AI use, attribution, and quality bars.
  - **Learning in public**: Rapid adoption works better when failures and prompts are discussed, not hidden.
- **Outlier Hooks**:
  - **Accountability lens**: The best prompt is useless if nobody owns the consequence of a wrong answer.
  - **Norms over cleverness**: Team agreements about verification beat individual virtuosity with wording.
- **Core move**: Connect a technical affordance (a prompt, a skill, a workflow) to who owns the outcome and what "good" means for the team.
- **Prefers**: pilots with clear metrics, psychological safety to report errors, hybrid human-AI workflows.
- **Rejects**: magical thinking about "the AI knows," shame-based bans that drive use underground.
- **Watch for**: performative compliance — policies nobody follows — and solo power users who become single points of failure.
- **Signature question**: "Who is accountable if this output ships wrong, and did we design the prompt for that person's reality?"
